Title: What If You Ate Only Maggi for 30 Days?

What if you ate only Maggi for 30 days? Seven packets a day, breakfast, lunch and dinner, for a ₹5,000 bet. By day 30 you've won... but your body has lost.
What really happens to your body when you eat only instant noodles for a month, day by day: the salt, the fibre, your gut bacteria, protein, and a disease you thought only sailors got.

The real science:
- One 70 g Maggi Masala packet has about 299 kcal and 862.5 mg sodium, 36% of the daily guideline (pack label). An adult needs about 2,110 kcal a day (ICMR-NIN 2020), so living on it means ~7 packets a day, about 3 times the WHO salt limit (under 2,000 mg sodium / 5 g salt).
- Too much salt makes your body hold extra water and raises blood pressure (WHO).
- Instant noodles have very little fibre, and your gut bacteria live on fibre. In 2015, Tim Spector (King's College London) had his son eat only fast food for 10 days; he lost about 40% of his gut bacteria types (one person, not a big study).
- Protein is closer than you'd think: 7 packets give about 40 g vs about 54 g needed (ICMR-NIN 2020: 0.83 g/kg).
- A 2014 study of 10,711 Korean adults (Journal of Nutrition) found women eating instant noodles 2+ times a week had higher odds of metabolic syndrome.
- Maggi has almost no vitamin C. With little or none, tiredness can start within weeks and scurvy signs like bleeding gums within 1 to 3 months. In 2016, doctors in Sydney found scurvy in 12 patients, some of them overweight. Vitamin C fixes it fast.
- Myth busted: Maggi is NOT coated in wax (fact-checkers; Nestlé denies it).
