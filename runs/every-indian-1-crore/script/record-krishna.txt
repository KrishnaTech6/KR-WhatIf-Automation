KR's What If Explained — "What If Every Indian Got ₹1 Crore Tomorrow?"
RECORDING SCRIPT (Krishna's own voice)

How to record (phone is fine):
- Quiet room, phone 15–20 cm from your mouth, same place for all three files.
- One file per script: main, short, reel (WAV or the phone's normal voice memo, both OK).
- Leave ~1 second of silence between sections (the [ ] headings). Don't read the headings or the notes in ( ).
- A mistake? Pause, and say the whole line again. I'll cut the bad take.
- (pause) = stop for about 1 second.  "..." = a short dramatic pause.
- [CUT] = stop, take a breath, ~2 seconds of silence, then go on.
- Mood notes like (tense), (playful) are just how the line should feel.
- Short and Reel: same words, just a little faster and punchier.
- Short ends mid-sentence ("...gets a crore") on purpose: it loops back to its first line. Don't add anything after it.
- Numbers and Hindi words (crore, lakh, didi, sabzi, Nashik): just say them your natural way.


==================== 1. MAIN VIDEO (~4.5 min) ====================


[Hook]  (tense)
Eight AM. Your phone buzzes. One crore rupees... credited. (pause)
Not just you. Every Indian. All one hundred forty-six crore of us.
And the person who loses the most... is sitting in your house.

[Promise - logo]  (neutral)
This is KR's What If Explained... and here's how it plays out.
Brand new money. No tax. One crore each... even every baby.
I went through seven reports, from the RBI to the UN, to see what happens next.

[Hour one - buzz]  (neutral)
Hour one. You check the app three times. It's real.
For the average Indian, one crore is about fifty years of income.
You call your mother. She's crying. (playful) She's already planning a wedding... she just doesn't know whose. (pause)
(neutral) Then you open a car website. So does everyone else in India.

[Hour six - showroom]  (tense)
Hour six. You walk into the car showroom near your house.
A few cars on the floor. A crowd at the door. Everyone has a crore. Nobody wants to wait.
So the manager does the only thing he can.
(deadpan) He changes the price tag. (pause)
(neutral) Today there's more money in India. There are not more cars.
(playful) So, comment in one word: what would you buy first?

[Day three - nobody comes]  (neutral)
Day three. Your doorbell doesn't ring.
The didi who cleans your house isn't coming. She has a crore too.
(tense) The delivery app shows no riders. NITI Aayog counted about seventy-seven lakh gig workers a few years ago. Today, almost none log in.
(neutral) The sabzi cart at the corner is gone.
(soft) You're a crorepati... eating dry bread, because nobody brought the milk. (pause)
(neutral) That's when it hits you. Money was never the thing. It was a promise that someone would work for you.

[Week two - the numbers]  (playful)
Week two. Time to answer a question every Indian kid has asked.
Why doesn't the RBI just print more notes, and make everyone rich?
(neutral) Here's why. All the money in India... cash, bank accounts, FDs... just got about forty-seven times bigger. In one morning.
Same tomatoes. Same houses. Same cars.
So prices climb... until they catch up.
(dramatic) I ran the numbers. Your crore now buys roughly what two lakh rupees bought last month. (pause)

[CUT]

[Month two - zimbabwe]  (deep)
Month two. And now... nobody trusts the rupee.
A farmer in Nashik won't sell his onions for notes anymore. He wants gold. Or rice. Or your phone.
This isn't a guess. It happened in Zimbabwe.
The government printed money to pay its bills. At the worst point, prices doubled roughly every day.
They even printed a note for one hundred trillion dollars. And soon, people stopped using their own money.

[Month three - grandmother]  (soft)
Month three. Remember the person in your house who loses the most?
It's your grandmother.
For forty years, she saved. A little every month. Ten lakh rupees in an FD.
Before that morning, it was her safety for the rest of her life.
(deep) Now it buys what about twenty thousand rupees used to. (pause)
She got a crore like everyone else. But a lifetime of saving... just melted.

[Payoff - already happening]  (dramatic)
And here's the part that's real.
A slow version of this is already happening.
The RBI's own target is four percent inflation, every year.
At that speed, prices double about every eighteen years.
(soft) Nobody is handing out crores. But a little of your grandmother's FD... melts every single year anyway.
(warm) Tell me in the comments: who's the best saver in your family?... Send this to them.

[Bonus - kyc]  (warm)
And the one thing that never lost value through all of this? The farmer's skill. Your skill. Money can be printed. That can't.
(deadpan) Eight AM. Your phone buzzes again. It's your bank... asking you to update your KYC.
(warm) Next time: what if India had a four-day work week?... But that story... is for the next What If.
