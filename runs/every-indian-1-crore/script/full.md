# What If Every Indian Got ₹1 Crore Tomorrow? — Script

Defaults used (Krishna picked #2 on 23 Nov, no options given): India · English · 4–5 min · no memes. Style: **collage**. Verticals: **standard captions** (the last two videos used bold).

## Package

Verdict: very relatable (every Indian kid has asked "why doesn't RBI just print more money?") and it was promised as the tease at the end of the 5 AM video. Other channels on this topic do lottery-style fantasy ("what I'd buy"). Our angle is a day-by-day story from inside your house. The real twist: your crore ends up worth about one year's income, the person who loses most is the saver in your family, and a slow version of this already happens every year.

**Effort beat count:** seven reports: UN/UNFPA 2025, MoSPI national accounts, RBI weekly supplement, the RBI inflation-target notification, the NITI Aayog gig report, Hanke & Bostrom (JHU), and Cato/JHU on Zimbabwe.

**Fact check:** a separate agent re-checked all 9 claims without the script or the sources. Result: 8 PASS, 1 PARTIAL. The partial was per-capita income: MoSPI moved to a new 2022-23 base in Feb 2026, which revised FY2024-25 to ₹1.93 lakh. "About fifty years" holds on both bases, so the line is unchanged and the fact sheet now shows both. It also suggested saying that the real outcome could be worse than the simple rule, because people rush to spend cash; Month two already says this ("And now... nobody trusts the rupee").

**Myth busted:** "Why doesn't RBI just print more notes and make everyone rich?" More money without more goods only raises prices (Zimbabwe, 2008).

## Full script

Story: one morning when every Indian's phone buzzes, seen from inside your house. Each chapter is a small scene: the call to your mother, the showroom, the empty doorstep, the question every kid asks, the farmer in Nashik, your grandmother's FD.

### Hook (0:00–0:16)

> Eight A M. Your phone buzzes. One crore rupees... credited. *(beat)*
>
> Not just you. Every Indian. All one hundred forty-six crore of us.
>
> And the person who loses the most... is sitting in your house.

- **Tone:** Tense, close.
- **Scenes:** (1) Frame 0: a paper-cut phone buzzing on a kitchen table at 8:00, the screen reading "₹1,00,00,000 CREDITED", with note bundles spilling out (this echoes the thumbnail). (2) Pull back: a torn India map, with phones lighting up across it like stamps, and a counter "146 CRORE". (3) A paper house in cross-section with four cut-out silhouettes in it and a red marker question mark circling the house.
- **Sound:** Phone buzz on frame 0, ticking + low heartbeat.
- **On-screen text:** "₹1 CRORE CREDITED" → "EVERY INDIAN" → "WHO LOSES MOST?"

### Promise - logo (0:17–0:35)

> This is K R's What If Explained... and here's how it plays out.
>
> Brand new money. No tax. One crore each... even every baby.
>
> I went through seven reports, from the R B I to the U N, to see what happens next.

- **Tone:** Neutral.
- **Scenes:** (1) KR logo sting (Night Atlas, ≤ 2 s). (2) A typewriter rules card on a torn strip: "NEW MONEY · NO TAX · ₹1 CR EACH" with a baby's rattle stamped beside it. (3) Effort beat: seven report cut-outs (RBI, UN, MoSPI, NITI…) land on a table with tape, counter 1→7.
- **Sound:** Logo sting, typewriter clacks, paper slaps.
- **On-screen text:** "THE RULES"; "7 REPORTS"

### Hour one - buzz (0:37–0:59)

> Hour one. You check the app three times. It's real.
>
> For the average Indian, one crore is about fifty years of income.
>
> You call your mother. She's crying. *[playful]* She's already planning a wedding... she just doesn't know whose. *(beat)*
>
> *[neutral]* Then you open a car website. So does everyone else in India.

- **Tone:** Neutral, playful joke, back to neutral.
- **Scenes:** (1) Chapter card "HOUR 1". (2) A thumb taps the bank app three times, the balance re-stamped each time. (3) 50 paper salary slips stacking into a tower, labelled "≈ 50 YEARS OF INCOME". (4) A phone call split: you and your mother (cut-outs, no faces drawn in detail); a wedding card with a blank name line pops up. (5) A browser "CARS" page with a red "VISITORS" counter spinning.
- **Sound:** Soft pad; bouncy pluck on "whose".
- **On-screen text:** "HOUR 1"; "₹1 CR ≈ 50 YEARS OF INCOME"

### Hour six - showroom (1:00–1:29)

> Hour six. You walk into the car showroom near your house.
>
> A few cars on the floor. A crowd at the door. Everyone has a crore. Nobody wants to wait.
>
> So the manager does the only thing he can.
>
> *[deadpan]* He changes the price tag. *(beat)*
>
> *[neutral]* Today there's more money in India. There are not more cars.
>
> *[playful]* So, comment in one word: what would you buy first?

- **Tone:** Tense, deadpan punch, light comment prompt.
- **Scenes:** (1) Chapter card "HOUR 6". (2) A paper showroom: three cars, and a crowd of cut-outs pressing at the glass door. (3) The manager tears off a price tag and tapes on a bigger one ("SOLD OUT" stamp lands next to it). (4) A split: a money pile growing vs. a car row that stays the same, joined by red string. (5) KR cut-out holding a comment card.
- **Sound:** Tense pulse; tape rip; stamp hit.
- **On-screen text:** "HOUR 6"; "MORE MONEY ≠ MORE CARS"; "COMMENT: 1 WORD"

### Day three - nobody comes (1:30–2:05)

> Day three. Your doorbell doesn't ring.
>
> The didi who cleans your house isn't coming. She has a crore too.
>
> *[tense]* The delivery app shows no riders. NITI Aayog counted about seventy-seven lakh gig workers a few years ago. Today, almost none log in.
>
> *[neutral]* The sabzi cart at the corner is gone.
>
> *[soft]* You're a crorepati... eating dry bread, because nobody brought the milk. *(beat)*
>
> *[neutral]* That's when it hits you. Money was never the thing. It was a promise that someone would work for you.

- **Tone:** Neutral, tense on the app, soft on the bread, a quiet realisation.
- **Scenes:** (1) Chapter card "DAY 3". (2) A doorstep with no footprints and a broom leaning on the wall. (3) A delivery-app map that empties: rider dots blink out one by one, with "77 LAKH" crossed out in red marker. (4) An empty street corner with a dashed outline where the sabzi cart used to stand. (5) A plate with dry bread and an empty milk packet. (6) A rupee note tied with red string to a pair of hands (worker, farmer, driver).
- **Sound:** Silence where the doorbell should be, tense pulse on the app, soft piano on the bread.
- **On-screen text:** "DAY 3"; "77 LAKH GIG WORKERS → 0"; "MONEY = A PROMISE"

### Week two - the numbers (2:07–2:40)

> Week two. Time to answer a question every Indian kid has asked.
>
> Why doesn't the R B I just print more notes, and make everyone rich?
>
> *[neutral]* Here's why. All the money in India... cash, bank accounts, F Ds... just got about forty-seven times bigger. In one morning.
>
> Same tomatoes. Same houses. Same cars.
>
> So prices climb... until they catch up.
>
> *[dramatic]* I ran the numbers. Your crore now buys roughly what two lakh rupees bought last month. *(beat)*

- **Tone:** Playful myth question, neutral explanation, dramatic reveal.
- **Scenes:** (1) Chapter card "WEEK 2". (2) A school notebook page in a child's handwriting: "Why can't RBI print more money?" (3) A balance scale: a small money stack vs. goods (tomato, house, car), then the money stack grows ×47 and tips the scale. (4) A tomato price tag counter racing up. (5) "₹1 CRORE" in typewriter type, struck through with red marker, and "≈ ₹2 LAKH" stamped beside it.
- **Sound:** Bouncy pluck on the question; a hopeful build; a dramatic hit on "two lakh".
- **On-screen text:** "WEEK 2"; "MONEY ×47"; "₹1 CR ≈ ₹2 LAKH"

### Month two - zimbabwe (2:43–3:13) — hard silence before

> Month two. And now... nobody trusts the rupee.
>
> A farmer in Nashik won't sell his onions for notes anymore. He wants gold. Or rice. Or your phone.
>
> This isn't a guess. It happened in Zimbabwe.
>
> The government printed money to pay its bills. At the worst point, prices doubled roughly every day.
>
> They even printed a note for one hundred trillion dollars. And soon, people stopped using their own money.

- **Tone:** Deep, slow, the biggest escalation.
- **Scenes:** (1) Black frame (1 s silence), then the chapter card "MONTH 2". (2) A Nashik onion mandi: a farmer cut-out pushes back a stack of notes, with a gold coin and a rice sack on the scale instead. (3) A torn newspaper headline: "ZIMBABWE, 2008". (4) A calendar strip where a bread price doubles each day (a counter). (5) A giant paper note "100,000,000,000,000" in a generic design (not a replica), with a "WORTHLESS" stamp. (6) Notes blowing away like litter.
- **Sound:** Hard cut to silence, then a dark drone + wind; a stamp hit on "WORTHLESS".
- **On-screen text:** "MONTH 2"; "ZIMBABWE, 2008: PRICES DOUBLED ~EVERY DAY"

### Month three - grandmother (3:14–3:44)

> Month three. Remember the person in your house who loses the most?
>
> It's your grandmother.
>
> For forty years, she saved. A little every month. Ten lakh rupees in an F D.
>
> Before that morning, it was her safety for the rest of her life.
>
> *[deep]* Now it buys what about twenty thousand rupees used to. *(beat)*
>
> She got a crore like everyone else. But a lifetime of saving... just melted.

- **Tone:** Soft, then deep and slow on the number.
- **Scenes:** (1) Chapter card "MONTH 3". (2) The opening paper house again; the red question mark lands on a grandmother cut-out with her spectacles and a steel tin. (3) A passbook whose pages flip through 40 years of small deposits. (4) An FD certificate "₹10,00,000" that shrinks and melts like wax into "≈ ₹20,000". (5) She sits next to everyone else's equal crore stack, and her own small stack is gone.
- **Sound:** Soft single-note piano; the drone stays under it.
- **On-screen text:** "MONTH 3"; "₹10 LAKH FD → ≈ ₹20,000"

### Payoff - already happening (3:45–4:14)

> And here's the part that's real.
>
> A slow version of this is already happening.
>
> The R B I's own target is four percent inflation, every year.
>
> At that speed, prices double about every eighteen years.
>
> *[soft]* Nobody is handing out crores. But a little of your grandmother's F D... melts every single year anyway.
>
> *[warm]* Tell me in the comments: who's the best saver in your family?... Send this to them.

- **Tone:** Dramatic reveal, then soft, then warm.
- **Scenes:** (1) A typed RBI-style notice card: "INFLATION TARGET 4%". (2) A tomato price tag ages through a calendar: today, then ×2 at "+18 YEARS". (3) The FD certificate again, melting a little at the corner every year (drip counter). (4) A comment card with a heart.
- **Sound:** A hopeful build turning bittersweet; soft piano.
- **On-screen text:** "4% A YEAR"; "PRICES ×2 IN ~18 YEARS"; "WHO'S THE SAVER?"

### Bonus - kyc (4:15–4:41)

> And the one thing that never lost value through all of this? The farmer's skill. Your skill. Money can be printed. That can't.
>
> *[deadpan]* Eight A M. Your phone buzzes again. It's your bank... asking you to update your K Y C.
>
> *[warm]* Next time: what if India had a four-day work week?... But that story... is for the next What If.

- **Tone:** Warm, deadpan joke, warm sign-off.
- **Scenes:** (1) The farmer's hands, a tailor's hands and your hands at a laptop, with a "PRICELESS" stamp. (2) Callback: the same kitchen table at 8:00 and the same phone buzzing; the screen now reads "UPDATE YOUR KYC". KR cut-out facepalms. (3) Tease: a calendar with Friday torn off. (4) End card.
- **Sound:** Bouncy pluck on KYC; an ominous rumble into the tease, then a hard cut.
- **On-screen text:** "PRICELESS"; "UPDATE YOUR KYC"; "NEXT: 4-DAY WORK WEEK?"

## Visual approach

- **Style: collage.** The topic is money, society and "what happens to us", and the style rule lists this exact topic as collage. The other style that could fit is cinematic (crisis, Zimbabwe), but most of the story is homes and people, not a countdown.
- **Signature moments per chapter:** paper cut-out props, typewriter headlines on torn strips, red marker circles and strike-throughs, rubber stamps ("SOLD OUT", "WORTHLESS", "PRICELESS"), red string from money to hands. Notes are generic paper ₹ notes, never replicas of real banknotes.
- **Verticals:** standard captions (bold was used on the last two videos).
- **KR moments (cut-out KR):** the rules card, the comment card after the showroom, "I ran the numbers", the grandmother reveal (a hand on heart), the KYC facepalm.
- **Three visual threads:** (1) The 8 AM phone on the kitchen table opens and closes the video. (2) The paper house with the red question mark returns in Month three to point at the grandmother. (3) The twist object, the FD certificate, sits quietly on the shelf in the Hour one call shot before it melts in Month three.
- **Chapter cards:** HOUR 1 · HOUR 6 · DAY 3 · WEEK 2 · MONTH 2 · MONTH 3
