TITLE: What If You Stopped Paying Your EMI?
LANG: en
KEY_BEATS: hook, keys, twist
HARD_CUT_BEFORE: keys

[Hook | tense]
You stopped paying your E M I.
In ninety-one days, the bank marks you a defaulter.
No court needed... the bank can come for your home.
And the scariest part? A paper you signed... on day one.

[Promise - logo | neutral]
This is K R's What If Explained. I went through fourteen R B I rules, laws and Supreme Court judgments for this.
Say you took a home loan of thirty lakh rupees.
Your E M I is about twenty-six thousand a month.
And this month... you just don't pay.

[Day one - the bounce | tense]
Day one. Midnight. You're asleep. The auto-debit knocks on your account... and finds it empty.
That same night, the bank's computer marks you overdue. Not a manager. A computer.
You wake up to two S M S. One: E M I bounced. Two: a five hundred rupee fine for that failed debit, plus G S T. That's the bounce charge, at a big bank like H D F C.
{playful} Your first fear: now they'll pile interest on top of this fine too.
They can't. Since twenty twenty-four, R B I says: a flat penal charge. No interest on top of it.
{deadpan} Small mercy. Your account is still empty.

[Day thirty - the calls | playful]
Day thirty. The calls start.
In movies, this is where the goons kick down the door.
In real life, R B I has rules.
A recovery agent can't call you before eight in the morning... or after seven at night.
No threats. No shouting. No shaming you in front of your family or friends.
{deadpan} So the first call comes at eight oh one.

[Day thirty-one - the score | tense]
Day thirty-one. You've missed a full E M I, and your loan gets a label. S M A one. Special Mention Account.
Every two weeks, lenders send updates to credit bureaus like CIBIL.
So now your report shows it. And your score starts to fall.
That new phone on no-cost E M I? Rejected. The credit card upgrade? Rejected.
And that record... is kept for at least seven years.
{neutral} So tell me in the comments: if your E M I bounced tomorrow... who would you call first?

[Day sixty - dad's phone | soft]
Day sixty. A phone rings again. But not yours.
It's your father's. Remember? He signed as your guarantor.
In Indian law, a guarantor owes exactly what you owe.
The bank can go straight to him... and his credit score falls with yours.
He never borrowed a rupee.

[Day ninety-one - bad loan | dramatic]
Day ninety-one. Your loan gets a new name. N P A. Non-performing asset.
In plain words... a bad loan.
You're not a customer anymore. You're a problem on the bank's balance sheet.
And now the bank can use one of India's toughest laws. The SARFAESI Act.
Remember? No judge needed.

[Month four - the letter | neutral]
Month four. A letter arrives. It's polite. And it's very clear.
Sixty days. Pay everything you owe... or the bank takes the house.
You read it twice. Then you put it in a drawer.

[Month six - the keys | deep]
Month six. (pause 0.4)
Bank officers at your door. They take possession. A notice goes up on your wall. (pause 0.4)
Your name. Your address. Printed in two newspapers... one in your own language. (pause 0.4)
The whole colony reads it... with their morning chai. (pause 0.5)
Thirty days later, your home can go to auction.
{neutral} One fair rule: if it sells for more than you owe, the extra money usually comes back to you.

[Twist - the signature | soft]
Now, that paper from day one. Here's the twist.
Not paying a loan is not a crime.
The Supreme Court said it in twenty nineteen: being unable to repay is not cheating.
You can't go to jail for being broke.
{dramatic} But on day one, you signed an auto-debit mandate. And when that bounces... the law treats it like a bounced cheque.
It can mean up to two years in jail. Rare. But real.
{deadpan} The loan can't put you in jail. Your signature can.

[Payoff - the call | warm]
Good news: the bank doesn't want your house. It wants its money.
Call the bank before day ninety. Ask for time, or a smaller E M I.
So next time the phone buzzes... you call them first.
Ever got that bounce S M S? Yes or no?
Next time: what if India and Pakistan never split? (beat)
But that story... is for the next What If.
