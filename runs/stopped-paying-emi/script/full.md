# What If You Stopped Paying Your EMI? — Script

Defaults used (no answers needed): India · English · 4–5 min · no memes. Style: **collage**. Verticals: **bold captions** (Short). Next-video tease (Krishna's pick): *What If India and Pakistan Never Split?*

## Package

Verdict: very relatable (about 1 in 4 credit-eligible Indians has a running loan, and everyone fears the "EMI bounced" SMS), and people search it ("EMI not paid what happens"). Most videos on it are dry lawyer talk. Our angle: a day-by-day countdown from one bounced EMI, plus the twist that the loan can't jail you but your auto-debit signature can.

Myth busted: "The bank secretly piles extra interest on your penalty" (not since the 2024 penal-charge rules). Second myth: movie goons at the door (RBI conduct rules).

The session's network blocks rbi.org.in / indiacode / indiankanoon, so each claim rests on 2 independent copies or reports of the official text.

## Full script

### Hook (0:00–0:16)

> You stopped paying your EMI.
>
> In 91 days, the bank marks you a defaulter.
>
> No court needed... the bank can come for your home.
>
> And the scariest part? A paper you signed... on day 1.

- **Tone:** Tense, close, quiet. Like a friend telling you what's coming.
- **Scenes:** (1) Frame 0: paper cut-out phone on a torn red strip, screen stamped “EMI BOUNCED”, buzzing (jit); behind it a cut-out house with a dashed padlock outline. (2) A paper calendar; a red marker counts to 91, a red “DEFAULTER” stamp lands on a loan file. (3) A courtroom cut-out torn away; the house cut-out, a red “AUCTION?” stamp hovers, not landing. (4) A signature on a form, circled in red marker; the camera pulls away before we can read it.
- **Sound:** Ticking + low heartbeat; phone buzz SFX; stamp thud held back.
- **On-screen text:** “EMI STOPPED” · “91 DAYS” · “NO COURT”

### Promise - logo (0:16–0:35)

> This is KR's What If Explained. I went through 14 RBI rules, laws and Supreme Court judgments for this.
>
> Say you took a home loan of ₹30 lakh.
>
> Your EMI is about ₹26,000 a month.
>
> And this month... you just don't pay.

- **Tone:** Neutral, confident.
- **Scenes:** (1) Logo sting (2 s). (2) Papers shot: a stack of 14 cut-out documents (RBI circulars, Acts, SC judgments) with a counter 1→14. (3) Index card: “Home loan ₹30 lakh · EMI ₹26,000/month”. (4) KR cut-out shrugs beside an empty wallet.
- **Sound:** Logo hit, paper shuffle per page.
- **On-screen text:** “14 rules, laws & judgments” · “EMI ₹26,000”

### Day one - the bounce (0:35–1:10)

> Day 1. Midnight. You're asleep. The auto-debit knocks on your account... and finds it empty.
>
> That same night, the bank's computer marks you overdue. Not a manager. A computer.
>
> You wake up to two SMS. One: EMI bounced. Two: a ₹500 fine for that failed debit, plus GST. That's the bounce charge, at a big bank like HDFC.
>
> Your first fear: now they'll pile interest on top of this fine too.
>
> They can't. Since 2024, RBI says: a flat penal charge. No interest on top of it.
>
> That helps a little. But your account... is still empty.

- **Tone:** Tense, then playful myth-bust, deadpan close.
- **Scenes:** (1) Card: DAY 1 · The bounce. (2) Clock cut-out at 12:00; a NACH arrow hits an empty bank-account cut-out, bounces off. (3) A typed ledger line: “OVERDUE” stamped by a paper computer (no person). (4) Index card ₹500 + GST. (5) MYTH stamp over a tangled interest spiral; RBI 2024 strip slides in, spiral cut straight into one flat tag “PENAL CHARGE”. (6) Empty wallet again.
- **Sound:** Bouncy plucks on the myth; stamp thuds.
- **On-screen text:** “OVERDUE” · “₹500 + GST” · “MYTH”

### Day thirty - the calls (1:10–1:36)

> Day 30. You're at your desk when your phone lights up. Unknown number.
>
> You don't pick up. It rings again. And again.
>
> In movies, this is where goons kick down your door.
>
> In real life, RBI has rules.
>
> That recovery agent can't call you before 8 in the morning... or after 7 at night.
>
> No threats. No shouting. No shaming you in front of your family or friends.
>
> So the next morning... the first call comes at 8:01.

- **Tone:** Playful, deadpan joke.
- **Scenes:** (1) Card: DAY 30 · The calls. (1b) Your phone on a desk, “UNKNOWN NUMBER” ringing, missed-call counter 1→2→3. (2) Movie-style goons kicking a door (exaggerated cut-outs) → torn away. (3) RBI rule strip: “8 AM – 7 PM only”, a clock with a window shaded. (4) Three crossed-out stamps: threats, shouting, shaming. (5) Phone rings, clock at 8:01. KR cut-out deadpan.
- **Sound:** Bouncy plucks; phone ring.
- **On-screen text:** “8 AM – 7 PM” · “8:01”

### Day thirty-one - the score (1:36–2:10)

> Day 31. You've missed a full EMI, and your loan gets a label. SMA-1. Special Mention Account.
>
> Every two weeks, lenders send updates to credit bureaus like CIBIL.
>
> So now your report shows it. And your score starts to fall.
>
> That new phone on no-cost EMI? Rejected. The credit card upgrade? Rejected.
>
> And that record... is kept for at least 7 years.
>
> So tell me in the comments: if your EMI bounced tomorrow... who would you call first?

- **Tone:** Tense, then neutral for the comment ask.
- **Scenes:** (1) Card: DAY 31 · The score. (2) A file tag labelled “SMA-1”. (3) Red string from the bank to a CIBIL-style report card; a two-week calendar flips. (4) Score meter needle drops. (5) Two “REJECTED” stamps: a phone box, a credit card. (6) Index card “Kept for 7+ years”. (7) KR cut-out with the comment question.
- **Sound:** Tense pulse; two stamp thuds.
- **On-screen text:** “SMA-1” · “REJECTED” · “7 YEARS”

### Day sixty - dad's phone (2:10–2:30)

> Day 60. A phone rings again. But not yours.
>
> It's your father's. Remember? He signed as your guarantor.
>
> In Indian law, a guarantor owes exactly what you owe.
>
> The bank can go straight to him... and his credit score falls with yours.
>
> He never borrowed a rupee.

- **Tone:** Soft, personal.
- **Scenes:** (1) Card: DAY 60 · Dad's phone. (2) An old keypad phone on a table, ringing; a father's silhouette cut-out. (3) A loan form, the “GUARANTOR” signature circled. (4) Red string from your score meter to his; both needles drop together. (5) Empty wallet in his hand.
- **Sound:** Soft single-note piano; phone ring muffled.
- **On-screen text:** “GUARANTOR” · “SAME DEBT”

### Day ninety-one - bad loan (2:30–2:51)

> Day 91. Your loan gets a new name. NPA. Non-performing asset.
>
> In plain words... a bad loan.
>
> You're not a customer anymore. You're a problem on the bank's balance sheet.
>
> And now the bank can use one of India's toughest laws. The SARFAESI Act.
>
> Remember? No judge needed.

- **Tone:** Dramatic.
- **Scenes:** (1) Card: DAY 91 · Bad loan. (2) Big red “NPA” stamp on your loan file. (3) The file slides from a “CUSTOMERS” tray into a “PROBLEMS” tray on a bank balance sheet. (4) Headline strip: “SARFAESI ACT, 2002”. (5) A courtroom cut-out torn away: “NO COURT NEEDED”.
- **Sound:** Tense pulse → impact on the NPA stamp.
- **On-screen text:** “NPA” · “SARFAESI ACT” · “NO COURT NEEDED”

### Month four - the letter (2:51–3:06)

> Month 4. A letter arrives. It's polite. And it's very clear.
>
> 60 days. Pay everything you owe... or the bank takes the house.
>
> You read it twice. Then you put it in a drawer.

- **Tone:** Neutral, quiet, a little dry.
- **Scenes:** (1) Card: MONTH 4 · The letter. (2) A typed letter: “Notice u/s 13(2): 60 days”. (3) Hand puts it into a drawer; drawer closes.
- **Sound:** Paper rustle; drawer.
- **On-screen text:** “60 DAYS”

### Month six - the keys (3:06–3:33)

> Month 6.
>
> Bank officers at your door. They take possession. A notice goes up on your wall.
>
> Your name. Your address. Printed in two newspapers... one in your own language.
>
> The whole colony reads it... with their morning chai.
>
> 30 days later, your home can go to auction.
>
> One fair rule: if it sells for more than you owe, the extra money usually comes back to you.

- **Tone:** Deep, slowest of the video, long pauses.
- **Scenes:** (1) Card: MONTH 6 · The keys (after 1 s hard silence). (2) Door cut-out, shadows of two officers. (3) Possession notice pasted on the wall, taped. (4) Two newspaper cut-outs, one in Hindi script masthead, your name typed in. (5) Neighbours' hands with chai cups holding the paper. (6) “AUCTION” stamp finally lands on the house (thumbnail echo). (7) A small ₹ arrow back to you: “Extra money → you”.
- **Sound:** Hard cut to silence, then dark drone + wind; stamp impact.
- **On-screen text:** “POSSESSION” · “AUCTION” · “EXTRA → YOU”

### Twist - the signature (3:33–4:06)

> Now, that paper from day 1. Here's the twist.
>
> Not paying a loan is not a crime.
>
> The Supreme Court said it in 2019: being unable to repay is not cheating.
>
> You can't go to jail for being broke.
>
> But on day 1, you signed an auto-debit mandate. And when that bounces... the law treats it like a bounced cheque.
>
> It can mean up to 2 years in jail. Rare. But real.
>
> The loan can't put you in jail. Your signature can.

- **Tone:** Soft opener, then dramatic, deadpan last line.
- **Scenes:** (1) Card: DAY 1 · The signature (the red-circled signature from the hook). (2) Supreme Court cut-out, typed strip: “2019: unable to repay ≠ cheating”. (3) Handcuffs cut-out crossed out: “NOT A CRIME”. (4) The circled signature resolves: “AUTO-DEBIT MANDATE”. (5) Red string from the mandate to a bounced cheque cut-out: “= CHEQUE BOUNCE”. (6) Index card: “Up to 2 years jail · rare”.
- **Sound:** Hopeful build turns tense; stamp; silence before the deadpan line.
- **On-screen text:** “NOT A CRIME” · “UP TO 2 YEARS” · “RARE. BUT REAL.”

### Payoff - the call (4:06–4:34)

> Good news: the bank doesn't want your house. It wants its money.
>
> Call the bank before day 90. Ask for time, or a smaller EMI.
>
> So next time the phone buzzes... you call them first.
>
> Ever got that bounce SMS? Yes or no?
>
> Next time: what if India and Pakistan never split? *(beat)*
>
> But that story... is for the next What If.

- **Tone:** Warm, hopeful, ominous tease at the end.
- **Scenes:** (1) House cut-out, AUCTION stamp peels off. (2) Phone buzzing again, same framing as frame 0 — this time your hand picks it up and dials the bank. (3) Index card: “Call before day 90”. (4) KR cut-out with the comment question. (5) Tease card: a torn paper map of the subcontinent stitched back together with tape. (6) End card.
- **Sound:** Soft piano; ominous rumble on the tease.
- **On-screen text:** “CALL BEFORE DAY 90” · “NEXT: INDIA + PAKISTAN NEVER SPLIT?”

## Visual approach

- **Style: collage** (Paper Collage). Money, banks, laws and family: typed notices, stamps ("OVERDUE", "NPA", "AUCTION", "REJECTED"), newspaper strips and red string from your loan to your dad's score fit best. **Cinematic** could fit the 90-day countdown, but the feeling is "what happens to us and our money", not danger. Note: the last two videos were also collage; Krishna can switch this one to cinematic at ✅1.
- **Short captions: bold** (Style 2). The Short is a fact-led day-by-day list; the last video used standard captions. Reel stays standard.
- KR moments: promise (empty wallet), Day 30 deadpan "8:01", Day 31 comment ask, Month 6 reaction (silent), payoff comment ask.
- Visual threads: (1) the buzzing "EMI BOUNCED" phone from frame 0 returns in the payoff, same framing, now picked up; (2) the dashed padlock on the house sits in every house shot until the AUCTION stamp lands, then peels off at the end; (3) the red-circled signature appears in the hook and quietly on the Day 60 form, before the twist reveals it.
- Thumbnail echo in the first shots: the "EMI BOUNCED" phone, the paper house with padlock, the red stamp colour (stamp held back until Month 6).
- Chapter cards: DAY 1 · The bounce / DAY 30 · The calls / DAY 31 · The score / DAY 60 · Dad's phone / DAY 91 · Bad loan / MONTH 4 · The letter / MONTH 6 · The keys / DAY 1 · The signature.
