Defaults used (no answers needed): India · English · 4–5 min · no memes. Style: **collage**. Verticals: **standard captions**.

## Package

Verdict: relatable (every family has seen it in its own photos), searched, and usually told as dry economics. Our angle: your own family photo getting smaller, and the twist that a shrinking India already exists inside our own cities.

Myth busted: "Below 2 children per woman means India is already getting smaller." It isn't, yet: momentum keeps it growing until the early 2060s.

Fact check: a separate agent re-checked all 18 claims: 15 PASS, 3 PARTIAL, all fixed (Kerala now "more than one in five", the 25 lakh drop is schools only, 2.1 is the standard replacement figure). This session's network blocks most news and govt pages, so each number rests on 2+ independent outlets reporting SRS / UNFPA / UN / UDISE+ data.

## Structure, voice and sound

Countdown ladder through the years: Today → 2036 → 2046 → 2050 → the early 2060s (hard silence) → twist: it's already here → payoff: the family photo. English, ~622 words, ~4:35.

What the data changed: retention data still isn't available (analytics permission missing). The best Shorts opened on "you" + a place or a big number (Frozen India, Himalayas, 1 mm raindrop), so both verticals open that way.

## Full script

### Hook (0:00–0:14)

> Your nani's family photo... probably had five or six kids in it.
>
> Yours has two. Maybe one.
>
> Keep going like that... and India starts to shrink.
>
> And you'll see it first... somewhere you'd never guess.

- **Tone:** Soft, close.
- **Scenes:** (1) Frame 0: an old sepia family photo cut-out, six kids in a row, taped to paper. (2) A newer colour photo slides in beside it: two kids; one kid's cut-out peels away. (3) Paper India map; red marker draws an arrow pointing down, the map shrinks a little. (4) A question-mark stamp over a blurred silhouette (a school desk, hidden).
- **Sound:** Ticking + low heartbeat; camera shutter on each photo.
- **On-screen text:** "6 → 2 → 1?"

### Promise - logo (0:16–0:28)

> This is KR's What If Explained. Real numbers, simple words.
>
> And this what if... isn't fantasy. It's the easiest future to predict.
>
> Because everyone who'll be sixty in 2050... is already born.

- **Tone:** Neutral.
- **Scenes:** (1) KR logo sting. (2) Typed headline strip "THE MOST PREDICTABLE FUTURE". (3) A crowd of baby cut-outs; a red string ties them to a "2050 · AGE 60" tag.
- **Sound:** Logo sting, typewriter.
- **On-screen text:** "EVERYONE 60 IN 2050 IS ALREADY BORN"

### Today - the magic number (0:29–1:05)

> Today. The magic number is 2.1.
>
> That's how many children each woman needs, on average, for a country to stay the same size.
>
> Two to replace mum and dad. A little extra, because sadly, not every child grows up.
>
> India's number right now? 1.9.
>
> Below the line... for five years in a row.
>
> Here's the myth. Most people think that means India is already getting smaller.
>
> It's not. India is still growing.
>
> Think of a train after the brakes go on. It keeps rolling for kilometres.
>
> Our brakes are on. But the train... is very, very long.

- **Tone:** Neutral, playful on the myth, deadpan last line.
- **Scenes:** (1) Card: TODAY · The magic number. (2) Big paper "2.1" with a mum, dad and two kids cut-outs stacked under it. (3) "1.9" index card slaps on top, a red line stamped "BELOW REPLACEMENT · 5 YEARS". (4) Myth card "INDIA IS SHRINKING?" stamped "MYTH". (5) A paper train with brake sparks, rolling on and on, the train is so long it runs off the page.
- **Sound:** Plucks; stamp thud; train brakes squeal.
- **On-screen text:** "2.1 = SAME SIZE"; "INDIA: 1.9"

### Year 2036 - Kerala (1:07–1:45)

> 2036. Ten years from now.
>
> Go to Kerala. More than one in every five people there... is over sixty.
>
> Picture a grandmother in a big house. Four bedrooms. Three of them locked.
>
> Her son is in Dubai. Her daughter, in Bengaluru.
>
> On Sunday, the phone rings. That's the family visit.
>
> Quick question. How many brothers and sisters do your parents have? And how many do you have? Tell me in the comments.

- **Tone:** Warm, a little sad; friendly on the question.
- **Scenes:** (1) Card: 2036 · Kerala. (2) India map, Kerala highlighted; 5 people icons, 1 turns grey with "60+". (3) A big Kerala house cut-out, three of four windows with paper padlocks. (4) Red strings from the house to "DUBAI" and "BENGALURU" tags. (5) An old phone cut-out ringing on a table. (6) KR cut-out with a family tree, comment prompt.
- **Sound:** Soft piano; phone ring.
- **On-screen text:** "KERALA 2036: 1 IN 5+ OVER 60"; comment question

### Year 2046 - the classroom (1:47–2:30)

> 2046. Remember where I said you'd see it first?
>
> Not a hospital. A classroom.
>
> Around now, India is expected to have more people over sixty... than children under fifteen.
>
> And it has already started. In just one year, our schools counted about 25 lakh fewer little kids.
>
> Japan is a few steps ahead. About 450 schools close there every year. Some shut the day their last two students graduate.
>
> The school bell still works. There's just nobody left to ring it for.

- **Tone:** Neutral, deadpan last line.
- **Scenes:** (1) Card: 2046 · The classroom. (2) Hospital cut-out stamped ✗, a classroom cut-out circled in red. (3) Balance scale: grey-haired cut-outs outweigh children. (4) Headline strip "25 LAKH FEWER LITTLE KIDS — IN ONE YEAR". (5) Japanese school cut-out, two students in graduation clothes walking out, a "CLOSED" sign swings. (6) A school bell on an empty desk row.
- **Sound:** School bell; chalk scratch; door close.
- **On-screen text:** "60+ > UNDER 15"; "JAPAN: ~450 SCHOOLS CLOSE A YEAR"

### Year 2050 - who pays (2:32–3:02)

> 2050. One in every five Indians is over sixty.
>
> Now here's the part nobody talks about.
>
> Pensions, hospitals, the person who sits with your parents at night... all of it is paid for by people who work.
>
> And the young are a smaller slice of India every year.
>
> Somebody has to look after all those grandparents.
>
> And that somebody... is you.

- **Tone:** Tense, then deep on the last line.
- **Scenes:** (1) Card: 2050 · Who pays. (2) Five people cut-outs, one turns grey "60+". (3) A pension book, a hospital bed and a night lamp tied with red string to one office-worker cut-out. (4) A pie of working-age people shrinking. (5) A pointing finger turns to camera: "YOU".
- **Sound:** Tense pulse.
- **On-screen text:** "2050: 1 IN 5 OVER 60"

### The sixties - the peak (3:05–3:42)

> The early 2060s.
>
> India reaches about 170 crore people.
>
> And then... for the first time in over a hundred years... the number goes down.
>
> Next year, fewer of us. The year after, fewer again.
>
> No announcement. No headline. Just... one empty chair at every wedding.
>
> By 2100, the UN expects about 150 crore. That's 20 crore fewer people.
>
> And some scientists think it could fall much further. A 2020 study in The Lancet said... just 109 crore.

- **Tone:** Deep, slow; neutral on the science.
- **Scenes:** (1) Hard silence, then card: EARLY 2060s · The peak. (2) Counter climbs to 170 CRORE, freezes, then ticks back. (3) A wedding hall of paper chairs, one empty chair under a spotlight. (4) Counter to 150 CRORE, "−20 CRORE" stamp. (5) Two papers: "UN 2024 · 150 CRORE" vs "LANCET 2020 · 109 CRORE".
- **Sound:** Silence → dark drone + wind.
- **On-screen text:** "PEAK ≈ 170 CRORE"; "2100: ≈ 150 CRORE"; "LANCET 2020: 109 CRORE?"

### Twist - already here (3:44–4:08)

> And here's the twist. This isn't really the future.
>
> In Delhi, a woman today has, on average... 1.2 children.
>
> In Japan, it's about the same. *(beat)*
>
> Delhi... already has Japan's family size.
>
> And Kerala and Tamil Nadu aren't far behind.
>
> The shrinking India we just imagined... already exists. In our own cities. We just haven't noticed.

- **Tone:** Wonder.
- **Scenes:** (1) Card: TODAY · Already here. (2) Delhi skyline cut-out with "1.2" tag. (3) Tokyo skyline cut-out with "1.15" tag slides beside it; a red "=" stamp. (4) India map with Kerala and Tamil Nadu lighting up. (5) Zoom out over the map: a few cities glow.
- **Sound:** Hopeful build.
- **On-screen text:** "DELHI 1.2 ≈ JAPAN 1.15"

### Payoff - the family photo (4:10–4:40)

> So, back to that family photo.
>
> Your nani's had six kids. Yours had two.
>
> Your grandkid's photo? Maybe one child... with four grandparents standing behind.
>
> That's not the end of the world. Fewer people can mean emptier trains, cleaner air, more space.
>
> But only if we get ready... now.
>
> So tell me honestly... one child, two, or none? What would you choose?
>
> Next time: what if every Indian family owned a car? *(beat)*
>
> But that story... is for the next What If.

- **Tone:** Warm, then ominous tease.
- **Scenes:** (1) The same old sepia photo from frame 0, same framing. (2) The two-kid photo. (3) A new photo: one child, four grandparents behind. (4) An almost-empty local train cut-out, blue sky. (5) KR cut-out with the comment question. (6) Tease card: a traffic jam of paper cars. (7) End card.
- **Sound:** Soft piano; shutter; ominous rumble on the tease.
- **On-screen text:** "6 → 2 → 1"; "NEXT: EVERY FAMILY A CAR?"

## Visual approach

- **Style: collage** (Paper Collage). This is about our families, our cities and what happens to us: family photos, headline strips, stamps ("MYTH", "−20 CRORE") and red string from cause to effect fit best. **Cinematic** could fit the slow countdown to the peak, but the feeling is personal, not danger.
- **Short/Reel captions: standard.** The last video used bold, and these verticals are story-led.
- KR moments: logo/promise, train myth-bust, comment question (siblings), "that somebody is you", outro.
- Visual threads: (1) the family photo from frame 0 returns in the payoff, same framing, now with one child; (2) a small red down-arrow on the India map sits in every map shot as the "shrinking" symbol; (3) a tiny Delhi 1.2 tag appears unnoticed on the map in "Today", before the twist.
- Chapter cards: TODAY · The magic number / 2036 · Kerala / 2046 · The classroom / 2050 · Who pays / EARLY 2060s · The peak / TODAY · Already here / THE PHOTO · Back to nani.
