TITLE: What If India's Population Started Shrinking?
LANG: en
KEY_BEATS: hook, sixties, twist
HARD_CUT_BEFORE: sixties

[Hook | soft]
Your nani's family photo... probably had five or six kids in it.
Yours has two. Maybe one.
Keep going like that... and India starts to shrink.
And you'll see it first... somewhere you'd never guess.

[Promise - logo | neutral]
This is K R's What If Explained. Real numbers, simple words.
And this what if... isn't fantasy. It's the easiest future to predict.
Because everyone who'll be sixty in twenty fifty... is already born.

[Today - the magic number | neutral]
Today. The magic number is two point one.
That's how many children each woman needs, on average, for a country to stay the same size.
Two to replace mum and dad. A little extra, because sadly, not every child grows up.
India's number right now? One point nine.
Below the line... for five years in a row.
{playful} Here's the myth. Most people think that means India is already getting smaller.
It's not. India is still growing.
Think of a train after the brakes go on. It keeps rolling for kilometres.
{deadpan} Our brakes are on. But the train... is very, very long.

[Year twenty thirty-six - Kerala | warm]
Twenty thirty-six. Ten years from now.
Go to Kerala. More than one in every five people there... is over sixty.
Picture a grandmother in a big house. Four bedrooms. Three of them locked.
Her son is in Dubai. Her daughter, in Bengaluru.
On Sunday, the phone rings. That's the family visit.

[Year twenty forty-six - the classroom | neutral]
Twenty forty-six. Remember where I said you'd see it first?
Not a hospital. A classroom.
Around now, India is expected to have more people over sixty... than children under fifteen.
And it has already started. In just one year, our schools counted about twenty-five lakh fewer little kids.
Japan is a few steps ahead. About four hundred and fifty schools close there every year. Some shut the day their last two students graduate.
{deadpan} The school bell still works. There's just nobody left to ring it for.

[Year twenty fifty - who pays | tense]
Twenty fifty. One in every five Indians is over sixty.
Now here's the part nobody talks about.
Pensions, hospitals, the person who sits with your parents at night... all of it is paid for by people who work.
And the young are a smaller slice of India every year.
Somebody has to look after all those grandparents.
{deep} And that somebody... is you.

[The sixties - the peak | deep]
The early twenty sixties.
India reaches about one hundred seventy crore people. (pause 0.4)
And then... for the first time in over a hundred years... the number goes down. (pause 0.4)
Next year, fewer of us. The year after, fewer again.
No announcement. No headline. Just... one empty chair at every wedding. (pause 0.5)
By twenty-one hundred, the U N expects about one hundred fifty crore. (pause 0.3) That's twenty crore fewer people.
{neutral} And some scientists think it could fall much further. A twenty twenty study in The Lancet said... just one hundred nine crore.

[Twist - already here | wonder]
And here's the twist. This isn't really the future.
In Delhi, a woman today has, on average... one point two children.
In Japan, it's about the same. (beat)
Delhi... already has Japan's family size.
And Kerala and Tamil Nadu aren't far behind.
The shrinking India we just imagined... already exists. In our own cities. We just haven't noticed.

[Payoff - the family photo | warm]
So, back to that family photo.
Your nani's had six kids. Yours had two.
Your grandkid's photo? Maybe one child... with four grandparents standing behind.
That's not the end of the world. Fewer people can mean emptier trains, cleaner air, more space.
But only if we get ready... now.
So tell me honestly... one child, two, or none? What would you choose?
Next time: what if every Indian family owned a car? (beat)
But that story... is for the next What If.
