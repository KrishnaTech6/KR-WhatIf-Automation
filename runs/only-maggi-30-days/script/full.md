# What If You Ate Only Maggi for 30 Days? — Script

Defaults used (autopilot): India · English · 4–5 min · no memes. Style: **atlas** (Night Atlas). Chosen by Krishna, 16 Nov 2026.

## 1. Package

Verdict: relatable (every hostel kid has lived on Maggi for a few days), real body science, a strong visual hero (the bowl). Heavily covered as "I ate only X for 30 days" vlogs, but nobody does it as a fact-checked day-by-day body timeline with an Indian twist. That's our angle.

## 2. Fact sheet

Note: this session can't open web pages, so each number was cross-checked through web search results from at least 2 independent sources, then re-checked by a separate fact-check agent.

| Fact in script | Number | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adult daily energy need | ~2,110 kcal (ICMR-NIN 2020, sedentary man) | ICMR-NIN RDA 2020 brief note (nin.res.in/rdabook/brief_note.pdf); NIN study guides |
| One 70 g Maggi Masala pack | ~300 kcal (third-party label data 277–310) | Open Food Facts (Indian barcode 8901058851304); FatSecret India; Nestlé Canada label 310 kcal |
| Packs to survive | 2,110 ÷ 300 ≈ 7 a day, 210 in 30 days | Calculated |
| Cost | 7 × ₹14 MRP ≈ ₹98 a day | BigBasket MRP ₹14; Mint 2025 |
| India's Maggi | 6 billion+ servings in Nestlé India's FY2023-24 (a 15-month year, Jan 2023–Mar 2024) | Nestlé India annual report coverage (afaqs, Deccan Herald) |
| Sodium per pack | ~730–860 mg (> 1/3 of 2,000 mg) | Open Food Facts; FatSecret (810 mg) |
| 7 packs vs WHO limit | ~5,600 mg sodium ≈ 14 g salt ≈ 2.8× the 5 g limit → "nearly three times" | WHO salt fact sheet; calculated |
| Very little fibre | ~1–2.7 g per pack vs ~30 g need | Open Food Facts; ICMR-NIN |
| Wax myth is false | No wax; Nestlé denies | Lead Stories fact check 2020; Yahoo/AFP fact check; Truth or Fiction |
| Tim Spector's son (one person), 10 days McDonald's only | lost ~40% (~1,400) of gut bacteria species, 2015 | The Conversation / WEF republish; Science News review of *The Diet Myth* |
| Protein | 7 × ~5.7 g ≈ 40 g vs 0.83 g/kg × 65 kg ≈ 54 g | Label data; ICMR-NIN 2020 |
| Korean instant noodle study | Shin et al., J Nutr 2014, n = 10,711; women ≥ 2×/week: OR 1.68 for metabolic syndrome | ScienceDaily (Baylor release); Healio |
| No vitamin C in Maggi | none listed on label | Open Food Facts label |
| Scurvy timeline | fatigue/weakness within weeks; bleeding gums, bruising, poor wound healing in ~1–3 months | Merck Manual; Cleveland Clinic; NIH ODS |
| Sydney scurvy cases | 12 patients, Westmead Institute, Prof Jenny Gunton, Nov 2016; some overweight/obese | Westmead Institute release; FoodNavigator; Taipei Times |
| Treatment | symptoms improve within 24–48 h, full recovery ~2 weeks | NIH ODS; Westmead (all cured with vitamin C) |

Myth busted: "Maggi is coated in wax and stays in your stomach for days." False. The real problem is what's missing (fibre, vitamin C).

## 3. Structure, voice and sound

Countdown ladder: Day 1 → Day 3 → Week 1 → Day 10 → Day 20 → Day 30, each step a new body system, ending on the twist (scurvy: full but starving). Data change: the last videos' Shorts that won opened on a concrete body/place image ("India Was Frozen", "1 mm raindrop"), so the hook opens on the body count (210 packets, 3× salt), not on a question.

Voice: talk to one friend at night, close to the mic, ~150 wpm, slower on numbers, deadpan on jokes, lowest and slowest on Day 30.

| Cue | Where |
| --- | --- |

| Bouncy plucks | Day 1, wax myth |

## 4. Full script

### Hook (0:00–0:14) · high
**Voiceover:**
> You've eaten only Maggi for thirty days. Breakfast, lunch, dinner.
>
> Two hundred and ten packets.
>
> Every day, nearly three times the salt your body should get.
>
> And salt... isn't even the scary part.

- **Tone:** tense, then soft on the last line
- **Scenes:** (1) the hero bowl, steam, fork lifting noodles, warm yellow vs cool blue rim light (thumbnail echo); (2) packets stacking into a tower, counter to 210; (3) salt pouring into the bowl, a "×3" stamp; (4) the torn calendar page "DAY 30" falls into frame, red glow
- **Sound:** ticking + low heartbeat
- **On-screen text:** 210 PACKETS · 3× SALT

### Promise (0:15–0:38) · low
**Voiceover:**
> This is K R's What If Explained. I went through twelve nutrition labels, studies and health reports for this one.
>
> Here's the deal. An adult needs about two thousand one hundred calories a day.
>
> One packet gives you about three hundred.
>
> So to survive on Maggi alone... you need seven packets. Every. Single. Day.

- **Tone:** neutral, friendly
- **Scenes:** (1) logo sting; (2) papers shot: 12 reports fan out with a counter (effort beat); (3) a calorie bar 2,100 filled by packet blocks of 300; (4) seven packets snap into a row
- **Sound:** sting, paper shuffle, block clicks
- **On-screen text:** 12 SOURCES · 2,100 kcal · 300 kcal · ×7

### Day one – the dream (0:40–1:05) · low/playful
**Voiceover:**
> Day one. Hostel room. Seven packets lined up like trophies.
>
> You're not alone, by the way. India ate over six hundred crore servings of Maggi in just fifteen months.
>
> Total cost? Around a hundred rupees a day. Cheaper than one pizza.
>
> Two minutes each, they say. Seven times. That's your whole day, sorted.
>
> Honestly? Day one feels amazing.

- **Tone:** playful, deadpan on "your whole day, sorted"
- **Scenes:** (1) chapter card DAY 1; (2) hostel room, packets on a shelf like trophies; (3) India map lights up with bowls; (4) ₹100 vs a pizza on a scale; (5) a clock doing 7 × 2 min; (6) KR thumbs up
- **Sound:** bouncy plucks
- **On-screen text:** DAY 1 · 600 CRORE SERVINGS · ₹100/day

### Day three – the salt (1:06–1:32) · high
**Voiceover:**
> Day three. You wake up... and your face looks puffy.
>
> Your rings feel tight. You're thirsty all the time.
>
> That's the salt. Each packet carries more than a third of your daily limit.
>
> Seven packets... and your body holds on to extra water just to dilute it.
>
> Your blood pressure has to work harder. You can't feel it. But it's happening.

- **Tone:** tense, then neutral explaining
- **Scenes:** (1) chapter card DAY 3; (2) mirror: face outline swelling; (3) a ring tight on a finger; (4) one packet fills a third of a "daily limit" jar, seven packets overflow it; (5) body outline filling with water; (6) a BP gauge needle creeping up
- **Sound:** tense pulse, water slosh
- **On-screen text:** DAY 3 · > 1/3 LIMIT PER PACKET

### Week one – the wax myth (1:33–2:01) · low/playful
**Voiceover:**
> Week one. You hit the bathroom... and nothing happens.
>
> Maggi has very little fibre. Your gut has almost nothing to push with.
>
> Now, your cousin on WhatsApp says: Maggi is coated in wax. It stays in your stomach for days.
>
> Fact-checkers have checked that one again and again. No wax. Nestle says the same.
>
> The problem isn't what's in the packet. It's what isn't.

- **Tone:** playful myth-bust, deadpan close
- **Scenes:** (1) chapter card WEEK 1; (2) bathroom door, a tumbleweed; (3) fibre meter near empty; (4) phone with a forwarded message bubble "WAX!!"; (5) "FALSE" stamp; (6) packet with dashed outlines of missing veggies (visual thread)
- **Sound:** plucks, stamp hit
- **On-screen text:** WEEK 1 · MYTH: WAX → FALSE

### Day ten – the bacteria (2:02–2:35) · high (wonder)
**Voiceover:**
> Day ten. And the ones suffering most... aren't you.
>
> They're the trillions of bacteria living in your gut. They eat fibre. And you've stopped feeding them.
>
> In twenty fifteen, a scientist in London, Tim Spector, had his son eat only fast food for ten days.
>
> By the end, about forty percent of the bacteria types in his gut... were gone.
>
> So here's my question. What's the longest you've ever survived on just Maggi? Tell me in the comments.

- **Tone:** wonder, then curious
- **Scenes:** (1) chapter card DAY 10; (2) zoom into the gut: a glowing city of tiny bacteria; (3) empty plates in front of them; (4) London pin, "Tim Spector, 2015" index card; (5) 40% of the bacteria fade out; (6) comment prompt
- **Sound:** hopeful build turning hollow
- **On-screen text:** DAY 10 · 2015 · −40% SPECIES

### Day twenty – the surprise (2:36–3:20) · low → tense
**Voiceover:**
> Day twenty. Here's something nobody expects.
>
> Protein? You're actually not doing that badly. Seven packets give you around forty grams. You need about fifty-four.
>
> Calories? Fine. You're not even hungry.
>
> But you feel tired. Heavy. Like your phone stuck at five percent.
>
> Because full... is not the same as fed.
>
> And this isn't only a thirty-day problem. A twenty fourteen study of over ten thousand Korean adults found something odd.
>
> Women who ate instant noodles twice a week or more had higher odds of metabolic syndrome. Belly fat, high blood sugar, high blood pressure... together.
>
> Twice a week. You're on seven a day.

- **Tone:** neutral surprise, tense on "tired", deadpan close
- **Scenes:** (1) chapter card DAY 20; (2) protein bar 40 of 54 (surprisingly close); (3) KR shrugs; (4) phone battery at 5%; (5) Korea pin + study card "J Nutr 2014"; (6) three icons joining: belly, sugar, BP; (7) "2/week" vs "7/day"
- **Sound:** tense pulse low
- **On-screen text:** DAY 20 · 40 g / 54 g · 2014 STUDY

### Day thirty – gums (3:23–3:46) · high (hard silence before)
**Voiceover:**
> Day thirty.
>
> You wake up tired. Again. Your legs ache on the stairs.
>
> Small things. Easy to ignore.
>
> But in thirty days of Maggi... you've had almost zero vitamin C.
>
> Keep going a few more weeks... and your gums start to bleed. Bruises show up from nowhere. Small cuts stop healing.

- **Tone:** deep, lowest and slowest
- **Scenes:** (1) black, then chapter card DAY 30; (2) alarm clock, a heavy body outline on the bed; (3) legs on stairs, ache pulses; (4) vitamin C meter at zero; (5) quick future flashes: toothbrush with a red drop, a purple bruise, a cut that stays red
- **Sound:** hard silence, then dark drone + wind
- **On-screen text:** DAY 30 · VITAMIN C: 0

### Twist – the sailors (3:42–4:12) · low → high
**Voiceover:**
> Here's the twist. This has a name.
>
> Scurvy.
>
> The disease that killed sailors on long sea voyages, hundreds of years ago.
>
> With little or no vitamin C, the tiredness can start within weeks. The bleeding... within one to three months.
>
> And it's not just history. In twenty sixteen, doctors in Sydney found scurvy in twelve patients.
>
> Some of them were overweight. They looked well fed.
>
> You can be full every day... and still be starving.

- **Tone:** neutral reveal, dramatic on "Scurvy", soft, deadpan close
- **Scenes:** (1) hostel room dissolves into an old sailing ship at night; (2) "SCURVY" typed; (3) calendar 30 days; (4) Sydney pin, "2016 · 12 patients"; (5) a well-fed silhouette with an empty vitamin meter; (6) the bowl again, full
- **Sound:** soft piano
- **On-screen text:** SCURVY · 2016 · 12 PATIENTS

### Payoff – the lemon (4:13–4:42) · low/warm
**Voiceover:**
> The good news? Give your body vitamin C again, and the signs usually start fading within days.
>
> So, back to that hostel room. Same Maggi.
>
> Add peas. An egg. One squeeze of lemon.
>
> Same two minutes. Completely different month.
>
> What's your secret Maggi add-on?
>
> Next time: What if you walked ten thousand steps every day for a year? But that story... is for the next What If.

- **Tone:** warm, playful, then ominous tease
- **Scenes:** (1) vitamin meter refilling; (2) callback: same hostel shelf, same bowl framing as the hook; (3) peas, egg, lemon drop in, colours bloom; (4) KR waves; (5) comment prompt; (6) tease: footsteps counter, end card
- **Sound:** piano, then rumble
- **On-screen text:** + PEAS + EGG + LEMON · NEXT: 10,000 STEPS

## 5. Visual approach

- **Style: atlas (Night Atlas)**, because this is "how the human body works" day by day (style rule: human body / everyday science → atlas). Collage could also fit (India food culture), but the core is body science, and the last video (EMI) was collage.
- **Verticals: standard captions** (the EMI Short used bold captions; never two in a row).
- **KR moments:** logo/intro, thumbs up Day 1, shrug Day 20, gut zoom reaction Day 10, wave at the outro.
- **Three visual threads:** (1) the hero bowl on the hostel shelf opens and closes the video; (2) dashed outlines of the missing foods (veg, fruit, lemon) haunt every packet shot; (3) a small lemon sits quietly on the shelf from Day 1 (the twist's fix), noticed only at the end.
- **Chapter cards:** DAY 1 – The dream · DAY 3 – The salt · WEEK 1 – The wax myth · DAY 10 – The bacteria · DAY 20 – The surprise · DAY 30 – Gums · THE TWIST – Scurvy.
- **Thumbnail pieces the hook echoes:** the glossy yellow bowl, the torn "DAY 30" calendar page, the red warning glow.
