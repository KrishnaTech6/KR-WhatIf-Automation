# What If You Woke Up at 5 AM for 30 Days? — Script

Defaults used (autopilot): India · English · 4–5 min · no memes. Style: **atlas**. Verticals: **bold captions** (Short is fact-led; last two videos used standard).

## Package

Verdict: very relatable (everyone has tried the "5 AM club" at least once) and heavily searched, but most videos are personal vlogs ("I woke up at 5 AM for 30 days, here's what happened") or productivity hype. Our angle: a day-by-day story with real sleep science and India's own sleep data, a myth-bust (it's partly in your genes), and a twist — the magic number is not 5 AM, it's 10 PM.

Effort beat count: six sources (Van Dongen 2003, Jones 2019, Daghlas 2021, AASM/SRS 2015, Wakefit GISS 2024, LocalCircles 2024 — used for background: 61% of Indians got under 6 hours of uninterrupted sleep).

Fact check: a separate agent re-checked all 9 claims with no script or sources (6 PASS, 2 PARTIAL, arithmetic PASS). Fixes applied: "six hours **in bed**" (Van Dongen measured time in bed), "tiny blackouts of attention" instead of "switching off for seconds" (PVT lapses are ≥0.5 s), "sleepiness barely **rose**", and Daghlas reworded to "people whose genes set their sleep one hour earlier" with the press-release 840,000 figure dropped (it is a genetic, Mendelian-randomisation study). This session's network blocks opening most journal/news pages, so every number rests on 2+ independent outlets.

Myth busted: "Every successful person wakes up at 5 AM." In reality your natural sleep timing is partly genetic (351 gene regions), and waking at 5 on 6 hours of sleep makes you worse, not better.

## Full script

### Hook (0:00–0:16)

> Two of you. Same five AM alarm. Thirty days. *(beat)*
>
> By day fourteen, one brain works like it skipped up to two nights of sleep.
>
> One difference between these rooms. Spot it before I do.

- **Opening idea: a "spot the difference" game (Krishna 18 Nov).** Split screen: two identical bedrooms, two of "you", the same 5:00 alarm. One tiny detail differs (the bedtime dial: 11 left, 10 right). The viewer gets a job in second 1; the answer comes on Day 21. The split screen returns each chapter as a left-vs-right scoreboard.

- **Tone:** Tense, close.
- **Scenes:** (1) Frame 0: the twin-bell alarm clock at 5:00, ringing and shaking on a bedside table, dark star-filled window behind, a small "30 DAYS" calendar page. (2) A brain icon inside a head silhouette dimming like a battery, "2 NIGHTS" tiles crossing out. (3) Close on the clock face: the hands frozen, a question mark over a second, hidden dial.
- **Sound:** Ticking + low heartbeat; alarm bell hit on frame 0.
- **On-screen text:** "5 AM · 30 DAYS" → "DAY 14: −2 NIGHTS?"

### Promise - logo (0:16–0:35)

> This is KR's What If Explained.
>
> Alarm at five. No snooze. Everything else stays the same. Same job. Same phone. Same late dinners.
>
> I went through six sleep studies and surveys to see what really happens.

- **Tone:** Neutral.
- **Scenes:** (1) KR logo sting. (2) Rules card: "ALARM 5:00 · NO SNOOZE". (3) Three icons slide in: office bag, phone, dinner plate at 10:30 PM. (4) Effort beat: KR flips through a stack of six papers, counter 1→6.
- **Sound:** Logo sting, paper flips.
- **On-screen text:** "THE RULES"; "6 STUDIES & SURVEYS"

### Day one - dark (0:35–1:04)

>

>

>
> Over a month, that's sixty hours. Free.

- **Tone:** Wonder, then playful, then neutral.
- **Scenes:** (1) Chapter card "DAY 1". (2) Delhi rooftops at night, a sunrise clock showing "sunrise 6:47" far on the right. (3) Quiet room, phone face-down, zero notifications. (4) KR in a tie, making the bed with a flourish, snapping a photo of the dark sky. (5) A clock sweeps 5→7, two hour-blocks glow; 30 calendar pages stack into "60 HOURS".
- **Sound:** Soft wonder pad; bouncy pluck on "CEO"; camera click.
- **On-screen text:** "DAY 1"; "SUNRISE: ~2 HOURS AWAY"; "+60 HOURS"

### Day three - bedtime (1:04–1:24)

> Day three. Eleven forty PM.
>
> You're in bed... with your phone.
>
> And you're not alone. In one big survey, fifty-eight percent of Indians said they go to sleep after eleven.

(4) A sleep bar 11→5 = 6 h, shorter than a 7 h outline (dashed "missing" ring on the lost hour). (5) Split: alarm clock arrow moved, bedtime clock arrow stuck.
- **Sound:** Tense pulse, low.
- **On-screen text:** "58% SLEEP AFTER 11 PM"; "6 HOURS"

### Day seven - feels fine (1:24–1:51)

> Day seven. You feel... fine. Honestly, a little proud.
>
> A yawn at four PM. A second coffee. Nothing serious.
>
> And that feeling is the trap.

> Hold on to it. We'll come back to it on day fourteen.

- **Sound:** Light pluck; tense pulse under "trap".
- **On-screen text:** "DAY 7"; "I FEEL FINE"; "YOUR ALARM TIME? ↓"

### Day ten - myth (1:51–2:24)

> Day ten. A reel tells you: every successful person wakes up at five.
>
> Movies love this. The CEO jogging in the dark. The topper studying before sunrise.
>
> But here's what the science says.
>
> In twenty nineteen, researchers studied the DNA of nearly seven lakh people.
>
> They found three hundred fifty-one spots in our genes linked to being a morning person... or a night owl.
>
> So if five AM feels like torture... that's not laziness. Part of it is written in you.

- **Tone:** Playful myth-bust, then neutral.
- **Scenes:** (1) Chapter card "DAY 10". (2) A phone showing a hype reel "5 AM = SUCCESS". (3) Movie-style montage: a jogger silhouette, a student with a lamp. (4) "MYTH" stamp → DNA strand unwinding, a counter to 351 with lit markers; "Jones et al., 2019 · 6,97,828 people". (5) A lark and an owl perched on the DNA strand.
- **Sound:** Bouncy plucks; record-scratch stop on "But".
- **On-screen text:** "MYTH"; "351 GENE SPOTS"; "~7 LAKH PEOPLE"

### The big one - day fourteen (2:24–3:00)

*(one full second of silence before)*

> Day fourteen.
>
> In two thousand three, scientists in Pennsylvania gave people just six hours in bed a night. For two weeks.
>
> By the end, their brains performed as badly as people who had skipped up to two full nights of sleep. *(beat)*
>
> Slower reactions. Tiny blackouts of attention, again and again.
>
> And the scariest part? They didn't feel it.
>
> Their sleepiness barely rose after the first few days.
>
> Remember that "I feel fine" on day seven? That was this.

- **Tone:** Deep, lowest and slowest; soft on the last lines.
- **Scenes:** (1) Chapter card "DAY 14" out of black. (2) A lab at night: beds, "6 H" labels, a 14-day calendar ticking. (3) Two bars: "6 h × 14 days" equals "2 nights awake", balance scale settling level. (4) A reaction-time test: a light flashes, the hand slows, screen blanks for a moment. (5) A flat "sleepiness" line vs a falling "performance" line diverging. (6) The "I FEEL FINE" sticky note from Day 7 peels off and falls.
- **Sound:** Hard silence → dark drone + slow heartbeat.
- **On-screen text:** "DAY 14"; "Van Dongen et al., 2003"; "= UP TO 2 NIGHTS NO SLEEP"

### Day twenty-one - the fix (3:00–3:23)

> Day twenty-one. Did you spot the difference? *(beat)*
>
> It's the small dial on the clock. The bedtime. Left room: eleven. Right room: ten.
>
> So you change one number. Not the alarm. The bedtime.
>
> Phone out of the bedroom. Lights off at ten.
>
> Ten to five is seven hours, the minimum sleep experts recommend for adults.
>
> The first few nights, you stare at the ceiling.
>
> Then it gets easier. Some mornings, you even beat the alarm.

- **Tone:** Warm, a playful line.
- **Scenes:** (1) Chapter card "DAY 21". (2) The hidden second dial from the hook flips: "BEDTIME 10:00". (3) Phone placed in a basket outside the door. (4) Sleep bar 10→5 fills the 7 h outline exactly (dashed ring closes). (5) KR staring at the ceiling, counting fans. (6) KR awake at 4:58, clock rings at 5:00, KR already smiling.
- **Sound:** Hopeful build starts.
- **On-screen text:** "BEDTIME 10 PM"; "7 HOURS ✓"

### Payoff - day thirty (3:23–3:53)

> Day thirty. Here's the twist.
>
> The magic was never five AM. It was ten PM. *(beat)*
>
> A twenty twenty-one genetic study found that people whose genes set their sleep one hour earlier had a twenty-three percent lower risk of depression.
>
> Not more sleep. Just earlier sleep.
>
> Five AM on six hours slowly drains you. Five AM on seven hours... might just give you your mornings, and your mood.

- **Tone:** Wonder, then neutral, then wonder.
- **Scenes:** (1) Chapter card "DAY 30". (2) The 5:00 clock slides aside, a 10:00 PM moon-clock rises in its place, glowing. (3) A DNA strand with a clock-hand shifting one hour earlier; a "−23%" mood bar drops. (4) Two identical sleep bars shift one hour earlier together. (5) Side-by-side: KR grey and drained (6 h) vs KR bright at sunrise (7 h).
- **Sound:** Hopeful build peaks.
- **On-screen text:** "5 AM ✗ · 10 PM ✓"; "−23% DEPRESSION RISK · Daghlas et al., 2021"

### Bonus - sunrise (3:53–4:28)

> And the bonus? Day thirty-one. Almost seven AM.
>
> You're on the roof when the sun comes up. *(beat)*

(4) Tease: an office calendar with Friday torn off, a "4 DAYS" stamp. (5) End screen.

## Visual approach

- **Style: atlas** (Cinematic Night Atlas) — this is "how your body clock actually works": everyday science of the human body, with Indian homes and the Delhi sky (night → sunrise) as the stage, which is exactly atlas's rim-lit sunset skies and dashed "missing" rings. Collage could fit (India's sleep habits) but the core is body science, and the night-to-sunrise light carries the story. Change it if you prefer.
- **Verticals: bold captions YES** — the Short is fact-led (6 hours × 2 weeks = 2 nights), the last two videos used standard captions.
- **KR moments:** effort beat (papers), Day 1 "CEO", Day 7 thumbs-up, Day 21 ceiling-staring, Day 30 drained vs bright.
- **Three visual threads:** (1) the bedside 5:00 alarm clock from frame 0 returns on Day 31 with the bed already made; (2) the sleep bar with a dashed 7-hour outline (the missing hour) appears on Day 3, Day 14, Day 21; (3) the twist's object, a hidden second dial (bedtime), sits quietly on the clock from the hook and flips on Day 21.
- **Thumbnail echo:** the first shots carry the 5:00 twin-bell clock, the star-filled dark-blue sky with an orange glow at the horizon, and the "30 DAYS" calendar page — in their own composition (bedside, window), not a copy.
