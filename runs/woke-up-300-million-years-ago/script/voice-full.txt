TITLE: What If You Woke Up 300 Million Years Ago?
LANG: en
KEY_BEATS: hook, fire, payoff
HARD_CUT_BEFORE: fire

[Hook | soft]
You wake up. A dragonfly is sitting on your chest. (beat)
Its wings are as long as your whole arm.
{tense} Outside, one tiny spark could set the whole forest on fire.
And the scariest thing here... is not the dragonfly.

[Promise - logo | neutral]
This is K R's What If Explained. Real science, simple words.
Nobody can really go back in time. But let's pretend you did.
Three hundred million years back. Before even the dinosaurs.

[Minute one - the air | neutral]
Minute one. You take a deep breath.
And it feels... strangely good.
Why? Because this air has way more oxygen than ours.
Today, about one-fifth of the air is oxygen. Here? Almost one-third.
Don't worry, your body can handle that.
{playful} But fire loves oxygen. Strike a match here... and it doesn't just light. It goes whoosh.
{neutral} Remember that. It matters later.

[Hour one - the forest | wonder]
Hour one. You step outside.
And something feels wrong. Can you spot it?
There are no flowers. Not one. No grass. No birds singing.
Flowers, grass, birds... none of them exist yet.
The trees look weird too. Scaly, like snake skin. As tall as a ten-storey building.
{playful} And dinosaurs? Sorry. You're way too early.
The first dinosaur won't show up for another seventy million years.

[Hour three - the giants | tense]
Hour three. Something moves near your feet.
It's a millipede. Like the one in your garden... except this one is longer than your bed. (beat)
{deadpan} Good news: it probably eats plants. Probably.
{tense} And above you, that dragonfly again. And it's a hunter.
{neutral} So why is everything here so huge?
Here's the cool part. Bugs don't breathe with lungs like we do. Air just slowly leaks into their body through tiny tubes.
More oxygen in the air... means the air reaches deeper. So bugs could grow bigger.
At least, that's the popular idea. Scientists are still fighting about it.
Quick question. Giant millipede or giant dragonfly... which one are you running from first? Tell me in the comments.

[Night one - the fire | deep]
Night one.
Clouds roll in. (beat)
Lightning hits a tree.
Now, remember the extra oxygen?
In our world, wet leaves just smoke. Here, even damp plants can keep burning.
The fire doesn't walk. It runs.
And we know this really happened. Coal from this time is full of tiny bits of burnt wood. Fires came back... again and again.
{tense} So the danger here was never the bugs. It's the air itself.

[Year one - India under ice | neutral]
Year one. You've had enough. You want to go home. To India.
But here's the strange part. India is not where you left it.
All the land on Earth is slowly crashing together into one giant piece.
And India is stuck far down south... under thick ice.
The proof is still there. In Odisha, there are big boulders that only moving ice could have carried.
So the middle of the Earth is a steaming jungle... and India is frozen.

[Twist - trees that never rot | wonder]
Now think about this. Why is there so much oxygen here in the first place?
Because when these trees die... they don't fully rot.
They fall into the swamp, get buried in mud, and over millions of years... get squeezed into coal.
Why didn't they rot? Scientists still argue.
Maybe the tiny fungi that eat wood hadn't figured it out yet. Maybe the swamps were just too wet.
Either way, all that buried wood is what pumped this air full of oxygen.

[Payoff - your light switch | soft]
And later, India's own forests went through the same thing. They became India's coal.
Today, about three out of every four units of India's electricity come from burning coal.
So that forest that almost burned you this morning? (beat) It's in your light switch.
And every time we burn it, the carbon those trees locked away goes right back into the sky. That's happening right now.
{warm} If you could bring one thing back from that world, what would you pick? Tell me below.
You wake up. Your own room. The fan is spinning... running on a forest three hundred million years old.
Next time: what if India got no monsoon for a whole year? (beat) But that story... is for the next What If.
