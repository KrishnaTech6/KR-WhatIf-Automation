## Full script

### Hook (0:00–0:15)

> You open your eyes... and a dragonfly as wide as your arms lands on your chest. *(beat)*
>
> The air is almost one-third oxygen. One spark, and the forest burns.
>
> And the most dangerous thing here... isn't the bug.

- **Tone:** Soft, close, then tense on line 2.
- **Scenes:** (1) Extreme close-up: your eye opens; a huge shadow crosses the light. (2) A 70 cm dragonfly lands on your blanket; wings span your arms (arm-length ruler ghosted). (3) Oxygen gauge needle swings from 21% to ~30%; a spark falls on leaves and flames race. (4) Dark swamp; something huge moves behind the ferns.
- **Sound:** Ticking + low heartbeat; wing buzz; whoosh of flames
- **On-screen text:** "~30% OXYGEN"

### Promise - logo (0:17–0:29)

> This is KR's What If Explained. Real science, real numbers.
>
> Nothing can send you back in time. But let's pretend.
>
> One day... three hundred million years ago. Before a single dinosaur.

- **Tone:** Neutral, friendly; honesty line lightly.
- **Scenes:** (1) KR logo sting. (2) KR in a time-machine bed, shrugging at the "physically impossible" label. (3) Calendar pages flip backwards into a counter: -300,000,000 years.
- **Sound:** Logo sting, then a low pad
- **On-screen text:** "300 MILLION YEARS AGO"; "Not physically possible"

### Minute one - the air (0:31–0:49)

> Minute one.
>
> You take a breath... and it feels great.
>
> Today, oxygen is twenty-one percent of the air. Here, it's around thirty. Maybe more.
>
> Your lungs are fine with that. Doctors only worry when oxygen gets well above this.
>
> Your campfire, though... Fire here is very enthusiastic.

- **Tone:** Neutral, then deadpan.
- **Scenes:** (1) Chapter card: MINUTE 1 · The air. (2) KR takes a deep breath, sparkles of oxygen; two pie charts: 21% today vs ~30% then. (3) Doctor's gauge with a safe zone; the needle stays green. (4) KR lights a match: it flares like a torch, eyebrows singed.
- **Sound:** Breath; match flare; plucks on the joke
- **On-screen text:** "TODAY 21%" "THEN ~30%"

### Hour one - the forest (0:51–1:19)

> Hour one. You walk into the forest.
>
> Except these aren't trees you know. Giant clubmosses with scaly trunks, thirty metres tall. Ten storeys. And tree ferns everywhere.
>
> No flowers. No grass. No birdsong. Just the hum of insects.
>
> And no, there are no dinosaurs. In movies, every prehistoric scene has a T. rex.
>
> In reality, the first dinosaurs show up about seventy million years after this morning.
>
> You're early. Very early.

- **Tone:** Wonder, then playful myth-bust.
- **Scenes:** (1) Chapter card: HOUR 1 · The forest. (2) Wide shot of scaly clubmoss trunks, a 10-storey building ghosted beside one for scale. (3) Ground level: ferns, no flowers, no grass; insect hum. (4) A T. rex cut-out slides in; a red "WRONG ERA" stamp. (5) Timeline bar: you at 300 Ma, dinosaurs a long way right at 230 Ma.
- **Sound:** Hopeful build; plucks on the stamp
- **On-screen text:** "30 m"; "FIRST DINOSAURS: +70 MILLION YEARS"

### Hour three - the giants (1:20–1:55)

> Hour three. Something rustles in the ferns.
>
> A millipede... longer than your bed. It's called Arthropleura. It eats plants. Probably.
>
> Overhead, a griffinfly called Meganeura. Seventy-centimetre wings. A hunter.
>
> Why so big? Insects don't have lungs. Air seeps in through tiny tubes.
>
> The classic idea: more oxygen let bigger bodies still get enough.
>
> But newer studies say... it may not be that simple. Scientists are still arguing.
>
> So, quick question. Which one would you run from first... the giant millipede, or the griffinfly? Tell me in the comments.

- **Tone:** Tense, deadpan joke, then curious explainer.
- **Scenes:** (1) Chapter card: HOUR 3 · The giants. (2) Ferns part: Arthropleura slides past next to KR's bed, longer than the bed. (3) Meganeura dives overhead; wingspan bar 70 cm vs a pigeon. (4) Cutaway: insect body with glowing breathing tubes, oxygen dots seeping in. (5) Two lab papers side by side: "Oxygen!" vs "Not so simple". (6) Comment prompt card: millipede vs griffinfly.
- **Sound:** Tense pulse; skitter, buzz
- **On-screen text:** "ARTHROPLEURA ~2.6 m"; "MEGANEURA 70 cm"; comment question

### Night one - the fire (1:58–2:22)

> Night one.
>
> A storm rolls in. *(beat)*
>
> One bolt of lightning hits the forest.
>
> Today, damp leaves just smoulder. In this air, even damp plants can keep burning.
>
> The fire doesn't creep. It runs.
>
> Coal from this time is full of fossil charcoal. Layer after layer. These forests burned... again and again.
>
> So it's not the bugs. It's the air.

- **Tone:** Deep, slow, lowest of the video.
- **Scenes:** (1) (Hard silence before.) Chapter card: NIGHT 1 · The fire. (2) Storm clouds; one lightning bolt. (3) Flames racing through damp forest faster than KR can run. (4) Cutaway: layered coal seam with black charcoal streaks. (5) KR backs away; the oxygen gauge glows red.
- **Sound:** Hard cut to silence, then thunder, dark drone + wind, crackling fire
- **On-screen text:** "FOSSIL CHARCOAL"

### Year one - India under ice (2:24–2:50)

> Year one. You want to go home. To India, say.
>
> Bad news. There is no India yet. It's a piece of a giant southern continent called Gondwana... far to the south, under the polar ice.
>
> The rocks still remember. In Odisha, the Talchir boulder beds were dropped by that ice.
>
> So this planet has steaming swamps at the equator... and glaciers where India will be.

- **Tone:** Neutral, map explainer.
- **Scenes:** (1) Chapter card: YEAR 1 · Where is India?. (2) Globe spins to 300 Ma: India's outline (dashed) inside Gondwana far south near the polar ice. (3) Close-up: Talchir boulder bed in Odisha today, with the glacier ghosted over it. (4) Split screen: steaming equator swamp vs icy India.
- **Sound:** Wind; ice creak
- **On-screen text:** "GONDWANA"; "TALCHIR, ODISHA"

### Twist - trees that never rot (2:52–3:20)

> Now, the twist. Why is there so much oxygen?
>
> Because these trees die... and don't fully rot. They pile up in swamps, get buried, and slowly turn... into coal.
>
> In twenty twelve, one team said the fungi that rot wood just hadn't caught up yet.
>
> In twenty sixteen, another said no. It was wet swamps and moving continents.
>
> Either way, all that buried wood is what filled this sky with oxygen.

- **Tone:** Wonder, curious.
- **Scenes:** (1) Chapter card: 300 MILLION YEARS LATER · The twist. (2) Trees fall into swamp water, get buried, layer by layer, squeezed into black coal. (3) Two papers: 2012 "fungi late" vs 2016 "wet swamps + moving continents". (4) Oxygen dots rise from the buried layers into the sky.
- **Sound:** Hopeful build
- **On-screen text:** "2012 vs 2016"

### Payoff - your light switch (3:21–4:07)

> And the forests of Gondwana, a little later, became India's coal.
>
> Today, about three-quarters of India's electricity comes from burning coal.
>
> So the forest that nearly killed you this morning? *(beat)* It's in your light switch.
>
> Every time we burn it, we put that ancient carbon... back into the sky. That's already happening.
>
> If you could bring back one thing from that world, what would it be? Tell me below.
>
> You open your eyes. Your own room. The fan is running... on a forest three hundred million years old.
>
> Next time: what if India got no monsoon for a whole year? *(beat)* But that story... is for the next What If.

- **Tone:** Soft, slow; warm at the end.
- **Scenes:** (1) Gondwana forests flow into India's coal belt map. (2) Bar: about 3/4 of India's electricity from coal. (3) Light switch clicks: inside the wire, the clubmoss forest glows. (4) Smoke carries carbon dots back into the sky. (5) Callback: your eye opens again, now in your own room; ceiling fan turning. (6) Monsoon tease: dry cracked field, empty sky; hard cut.
- **Sound:** Soft piano; ominous rumble on the tease
- **On-screen text:** "~3/4 OF INDIA'S POWER"; comment question; next video title
