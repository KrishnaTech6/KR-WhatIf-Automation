# What If You Didn't Use Your Phone for 30 Days? — Script

Defaults used (no answers needed): India · English · 4–5 min · no memes. Style: **atlas**. Verticals: **standard captions**.

## Package

Verdict: very relatable (everyone has thought about a "digital detox"), heavily searched, but most videos are personal vlogs ("I quit my phone for 30 days"). Our angle: a day-by-day story with real numbers, India's own problem (UPI, OTP, train tickets all live on the phone), and a research twist — the phone was never the problem, the internet in it was.

Myth busted: "Throw your phone away and life is instantly better." In reality the first days are the hardest and only about a quarter of people kept mobile internet off for even 10 of 14 days.

## Full script

### Hook (0:00–0:15)

> Thirty days without your phone... and you get back 150 hours. Six full days. *(beat)*
>
> By day two, you're locked out of your own life.
>
> And the biggest change... happens inside your head.

- **Tone:** Tense, close.
- **Scenes:** (1) Frame 0: a smartphone dropping into a wooden drawer, the drawer slides shut; six glowing "DAY" tiles fly out of it. (2) A lock icon snapping shut over a wallet, a ticket and a map pin. (3) KR's head silhouette with a soft light switching on inside.
- **Sound:** Ticking + heartbeat; drawer thud.
- **On-screen text:** "30 DAYS · NO PHONE" → "+6 DAYS BACK"

### Promise - logo (0:15–0:27)

> This is KR's What If Explained. Real numbers, simple words.
>
> Here's the deal. Your smartphone goes in a drawer. Locked. For 30 days.
>
> No scrolling. No apps. No internet in your pocket.

- **Tone:** Neutral.
- **Scenes:** (1) KR logo sting. (2) The drawer with a padlock, a "30" calendar page. (3) Rules card: "NO SCROLLING · NO APPS · NO INTERNET".
- **Sound:** Logo sting, lock click.
- **On-screen text:** "THE RULES"

### Hour one - the reflex (0:28–0:58)

> Hour one. Your hand goes to your pocket. Nothing there.
>
> Ten minutes later... again.
>
> You're in a queue, and your thumb is scrolling... nothing. Just air.
>
> Then your leg buzzes. Your phone is in a drawer at home.
>
> That's called phantom vibration. In one study, 89% of students felt it.
>
> Your brain has been trained so well... it's now making up notifications.
>
> Thirty days, it says. Easy. It's been one hour.

- **Tone:** Playful, deadpan on "It's been one hour."
- **Scenes:** (1) Chapter card "HOUR 1". (2) A hand patting an empty pocket, dashed phone outline. (3) A thumb swiping in the air in a queue. (4) Buzz lines around a jeans pocket; a fake notification bubble pops and bursts. (5) Counter 0 → 89% "felt it".
- **Sound:** Plucks, a buzz SFX, bubble pop.
- **On-screen text:** "HOUR 1"; "89% FELT IT (Drouin, 2012)"

### Day two - the wall (0:59–1:35)

> Day two. And India hits you with a problem nobody warns you about.
>
> You need to pay someone. Your UPI app is in the drawer.
>
> You need a train ticket. Almost nine out of ten reserved tickets are now booked online.
>
> You're in a new part of the city. No maps. No idea where you are.
>
> Then your bank sends an OTP... to a phone you can't touch.
>
> So you walk into a mobile shop and buy the one phone that can't scroll. An old keypad phone. Calls and SMS. That's it.
>
> But now you see it. Your phone was never just a phone.
>
> It's your wallet, your ticket counter, your map and your bank.
>
> So you carry cash. You ask strangers for directions. Like it's 2005.

- **Tone:** Tense, light relief on the OTP joke.
- **Scenes:** (1) Card "DAY 2". (2) A shop QR sticker with a red cross; a map with a lost dot. (4b) Mobile shop counter: a keypad phone under a spotlight. (3) Train ticket bar: 9 of 10 ticket icons light up "ONLINE". (4) Keypad phone screen "OTP 4821" with a tick. (5) The phone outline splits into 4 icons: wallet, ticket, map pin, bank. (6) A paper map and coins, sepia tint "2005".
- **Sound:** Tense pulse; cash-register ding on "cash".
- **On-screen text:** "DAY 2"; "~9 IN 10 TRAIN TICKETS: ONLINE"

### Day seven - the boredom (1:36–2:20)

> Day seven. The hardest day.
>
> The average Indian spends about 5 hours a day on a phone.
>
> That's five hours you now have to fill. Every single day.
>
> The metro feels longer. The lift feels longer. Even the bathroom feels longer.
>
> You start reading shampoo bottles.
>
> But slowly, something else creeps in. You look up. You notice your own street. You finish a whole conversation without checking anything.
>
> Quick question. What's the first thing you'd do with five free hours a day? Tell me in the comments.

- **Tone:** Neutral, deadpan joke, then warm.
- **Scenes:** (1) Card "DAY 7". (2) A 24-hour clock ring with a 5-hour slice glowing. (3) Metro carriage, everyone on phones except you. (4) KR reading a shampoo bottle (KR moment: joke). (5) Street at sunset, you looking up, a chai with a friend. (6) Comment prompt card.
- **Sound:** Clock ticks, plucks on the joke, warm pad.
- **On-screen text:** "DAY 7"; "5 HRS/DAY (EY, 2025)"; comment question

### Day ten - the myth (2:21–2:55)

> Day ten. Here's the myth.
>
> In movies, someone throws their phone in a lake, smiles... and life is suddenly perfect.
>
> In real life, most people don't even make it this far.
>
> In a 2025 study, people blocked the internet on their phones for just two weeks.
>
> Only about a quarter lasted even 10 of those 14 days.
>
> The first week is the hardest. Your brain keeps asking for its little hits. And boredom... feels almost like pain.
>
> So if you're still going on day ten... you're already rare.

- **Tone:** Playful myth-bust → neutral.
- **Scenes:** (1) Card "DAY 10". (2) "IN MOVIES" frame: phone arcing into a lake, a sparkle smile; stamp "MYTH". (3) "IN REAL LIFE": 4 people icons, 3 fade out, 1 stays. (4) Paper card "PNAS Nexus, 2025". (5) A brain with small "ping" bubbles asking.
- **Sound:** Plucks, splash, record-scratch on "MYTH".
- **On-screen text:** "DAY 10"; "MYTH"; "~1 IN 4 LASTED 10 DAYS"

### Day fourteen - the brain (2:58–3:35)

> Day fourteen. *(beat)*
>
> And this is where it gets strange.
>
> That same study tested people's focus. One boring task. Stay on it. Don't drift.
>
> After two weeks without mobile internet, their focus improved... as much as being about ten years younger.
>
> Ten years. No pill. No app.
>
> Just... less scrolling.
>
> Nine out of ten people got better at something. Their mood, their focus, or how they felt about life.

- **Tone:** Deep, lowest and slowest; KR moment (big escalation).
- **Scenes:** (1) Hard silence, then card "DAY 14". (2) A focus test: a dot that must be watched, distractions flicker. (3) An age dial rolling back 10 years, KR shocked. (4) 10 people icons, 9 light up.
- **Sound:** Hard cut to silence, then drone + slow heartbeat.
- **On-screen text:** "DAY 14"; "FOCUS: ~10 YEARS YOUNGER"; "9 IN 10 IMPROVED"

### Day thirty - the twist (3:36–4:20)

> Day thirty. Let's count.
>
> Five hours a day, for thirty days. That's a hundred and fifty hours.
>
> Six full days of your life... handed back.
>
> That's almost a full week of holidays.
>
> And here's the twist.
>
> The people in that study? They never gave up their phones.
>
> Calls worked. Texts worked. Only the internet was gone.
>
> The phone was never the problem. The endless scroll was.
>
> And there's more. In a big Stanford study, people who quit Facebook for four weeks... kept using it less, even after they came back.
>
> A month doesn't just give you time back. It breaks the habit.

- **Tone:** Wonder, playful on "endless scroll", neutral close.
- **Scenes:** (1) Card "DAY 30". (2) Counter 0 → 150 hours, folding into 6 day tiles (KR moment: calculation). (3) The study's phone: call ✓, SMS ✓, a globe icon crossed out. (4) An endless scroll feed spinning, then snapping. (5) Paper card "Allcott et al., AER 2020"; a usage bar shrinking after "back".
- **Sound:** Hopeful build.
- **On-screen text:** "DAY 30"; "150 HRS = 6 DAYS"; "THE PROBLEM: THE SCROLL"

### Payoff - day thirty-one (4:21–4:50)

> Day thirty-one. You open the drawer.
>
> Same phone. Same lock screen. Hundreds of notifications.
>
> And for the first time in years... you don't rush to check them.
>
> Your hand goes to your pocket. And this time... it waits.
>
> So tell me honestly. Could you last thirty days? Or even three?
>
> Next time: what if you saved ₹100 every day for 20 years? *(beat)*
>
> But that story... is for the next What If.

- **Tone:** Warm; KR outro.
- **Scenes:** (1) The same drawer from frame 0 opens (callback). (2) Lock screen flooding with bubbles. (3) The hand at the pocket, pausing. (4) Comment prompt. (5) Tease: a piggy bank with a ₹100 note dropping in, a stack growing over 20 calendar pages. (6) End screen.
- **Sound:** Soft single-note piano; rumble on "Next time".
- **On-screen text:** "DAY 31"; "NEXT: ₹100 A DAY FOR 20 YEARS?"

## Visual approach

- **Style: atlas** (Cinematic Night Atlas) — the topic is how a habit, your brain and India's phone-based systems actually work; the style README lists "no phone for 30 days" under atlas, and the last video was collage, so this keeps variety. Collage could fit (people, everyday India) but the brain/focus science reads better as clean illustrated scenes.
- **Bold captions on the Short/Reel: no** (UPI just used them; never two in a row).
- **KR moments:** intro, the shampoo-bottle joke (Day 7), the 150-hour calculation (Day 30), the Day 14 shock, the outro.
- **Three visual threads:** (1) the wooden drawer opens the video and opens again on Day 31; (2) a dashed phone outline (the missing phone) sits in every relevant shot; (3) the keypad phone with "CALLS ✓ SMS ✓" quietly appears early, then becomes the twist (the study kept calls and texts).
- **Chapter cards:** HOUR 1 · Reflex / DAY 2 · The wall / DAY 7 · Boredom / DAY 10 · The myth / DAY 14 · The brain / DAY 30 · The twist / DAY 31 · Back.
