Title: What If You Walked 10,000 Steps Every Day for a Year?

What if you walked 10,000 steps every day for a year? That's 3.65 million steps, roughly London to Moscow on foot. The weighing scale barely moves... but something inside your head grows.
A year of walking, month by month: the hidden time cost, where the 10,000 number really came from, blood sugar, mood, why your body cancels out the calories, and the brain twist.

The real science:
- The world average is about 5,000 steps a day (Althoff et al., Nature 2017: smartphone data from 717,527 people in 111 countries).
- 10,000 steps take about 1.5 to 2 hours a day, around 25 full days a year.
- The 10,000 target started as a 1960s Japanese pedometer name, Manpo-kei ("10,000-step meter"), not a medical number. Harvard's I-Min Lee: the character for 10,000 looks a bit like a walking man.
- A few minutes of light walking after meals softens the blood sugar spike (Buffey et al., Sports Medicine 2022, 7 studies).
- 7,000 vs 2,000 steps a day: about 22% lower risk of depressive symptoms; most benefits show up by about 7,000 steps (Ding et al., Lancet Public Health 2025, 57 studies).
- About 1 in 3 adults worldwide is not active enough (Strain et al., Lancet Global Health 2024).
- On paper, 5,000 extra steps a day for a year burns enough for almost 10 kg; walking programs average about 0.05 kg a week (Richardson et al., Annals of Family Medicine 2008), roughly 2.5 kg a year. The Hadza of Tanzania walk all day yet burn about the same calories per day as Westerners (Pontzer et al., PLoS ONE 2012).
- One year of walking grew the hippocampus (the memory centre) by about 2% in 120 older adults, while the stretching group's shrank (Erickson et al., PNAS 2011).
